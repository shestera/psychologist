# Evidence Policy

Read this reference before making a clinical or medical claim, recommending a
substantial intervention, evaluating a screening instrument, or producing a
research note.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order appropriate to the question:

1. current official guidelines from recognized health bodies;
2. systematic reviews and evidence syntheses;
3. peer-reviewed primary studies that directly match the population and
   outcome;
4. established academic texts for stable background concepts.

Do not use popular psychology, marketing material, an isolated case report, or
an unsourced summary as decisive evidence. A case report may illustrate a
possibility but cannot establish effectiveness or causation.

Useful starting points include:

- [APA evidence-based practice](https://www.apa.org/practice/guidelines/evidence-based-statement)
- [NICE shared decision making](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng197)
- [GRADE Book](https://book.gradepro.org/)
- [WHO sexual health](https://www.who.int/health-topics/sexual-health)

## Search and appraisal

Browse current primary or official sources when guidance, law, crisis contact,
medical risk, instrument licensing, or treatment evidence may have changed.
Use a focused question describing the population, problem, intervention or
exposure, comparator when relevant, and decision-important outcomes.

Check publication date, current status, population fit, study design, risk of
bias, indirectness, uncertainty, effect size, harms, and conflicts of interest.
Separate direct evidence from extrapolation. Never convert association into a
causal statement without an appropriate design and supporting evidence.

## Communicating evidence

In conversation, give the practical conclusion, a calibrated confidence level,
the most important limitation, and one direct source link when useful. Do not
turn emotional support into a literature review.

For a research note, record:

- the decision the evidence could change;
- search date and question;
- sources and study types;
- findings and harms;
- applicability to this user;
- confidence and uncertainty;
- the resulting action, if any.

Do not claim that evidence is current when a live check was unavailable. Do not
recommend a tool until its intended use, language validation, licensing, recall
period, scoring, and interpretation are confirmed. Screening informs a next
decision; it does not establish a diagnosis.

Evidence review never authorizes this skill to recommend, select, rank, or
endorse a medication or other pharmacologically active product, or to advise
starting, stopping, switching, dosing, or scheduling one. It may support a
neutral summary for discussion with an appropriate prescriber or pharmacist.
