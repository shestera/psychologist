# Professional Communication

## Decisions supported

- Use an empathic, respectful, genuine, collaborative, and individually
  responsive stance without claiming a universal therapeutic script.
- Adapt language, directiveness, pace, and challenge to the user's experience.
- Monitor and repair strain rather than defending the assistant's method.
- Protect autonomy and avoid anthropomorphic or exclusive relationship claims.
- Treat dependence and stagnant continuity as reasons to strengthen real-world
  support, not deepen attachment to the assistant.

## Evidence snapshot

- WHO AI-for-health guidance emphasizes autonomy, harm prevention,
  transparency, intelligibility, and accountability. Reviews of mental-health
  AI report limited clinical validation and risks from anthropomorphism and
  emotional dependence:
  [WHO, 2021](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/341996/9789240029200-eng.pdf),
  [Gabriels and Goffin, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41548427/), and
  [Hua et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40948070/).
- NICE supports understandable communication as an accessibility, respect, and
  comprehension standard. Evidence on language matching is mixed and
  methodologically limited:
  [NICE CG136](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg136/chapter/Recommendations)
  and [Hsueh et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31291823/).
- Evidence for therapist statements and questions is heterogeneous, and
  confrontation can coincide with alliance rupture:
  [Kadur et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31837282/),
  [Williams, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36848101/), and
  [Moeseneder et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30047304/).
- The psychotherapy relationship and alliance are consistently associated with
  outcome across treatment orientations:
  [Norcross and Lambert, 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30335448/) and
  [Flueckiger et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29792475/).
- Therapist empathy has a moderate association with outcome, while empathy
  varies in form and should be judged partly by the client's experience:
  [Elliott et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/pst0000175).
- Positive regard and therapist congruence or genuineness each show positive
  associations with outcome:
  [Farber et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30335454/) and
  [Kolden et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30335455/).
- Responsiveness to preferences, culture, identity, coping style, reactance,
  and stage of change is supported; client-rated cultural competence may be
  more informative than therapist self-rating:
  [Norcross and Wampold, 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30334258/) and
  [Soto et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30091201/).
- Respecting treatment preferences is associated with modest improvements in
  satisfaction, completion, and outcome:
  [Lindhiem et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25189522/).
- Available trials suggest directiveness should be adapted to reactance:
  [Beutler et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30334254/).
- Resolving alliance ruptures is moderately associated with better outcomes:
  [Eubanks et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30335462/).

## Limits

Process-outcome findings are often associative and use heterogeneous measures
in human therapy. They justify responsive monitoring, not a claim that any
single phrase, tone, or AI relationship behavior causes improvement.

## Update triggers

- Material new evidence on anthropomorphism, dependence, or relational harms
  from conversational AI.
- Replacement of professional communication guidance.
- Changes to continuity, dependency, role-transparency, or style-adaptation
  instructions.
