# Psychometric Instrument Evidence

## Decisions supported

- Keep the runtime registry as a dated starting point, not a questionnaire
  library or default battery.
- Require intended use, population and language validation, recall period,
  scoring, interpretation, burden, and current rights to be checked before use.
- Treat every screening score as decision support rather than diagnosis,
  causation, identity, consent, or individual risk prediction.
- Make clear that the plugin's commercial license does not grant rights to
  third-party noncommercial or otherwise restricted instruments.

## Instrument provenance

### WHO-5

WHO supplies the official instrument, translations, scope, and CC BY-NC-SA 3.0
terms. Translation availability does not establish population-specific
validation:
[WHO-5 instrument page](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/WHO-UCN-MSD-MHE-2024.01).

### PHQ-9

The original criterion study included United States primary-care and
obstetrics-gynaecology settings, with an independent mental-health interview in
a subset. Extrapolation to other ages, settings, languages, and populations
requires separate validation:
[Kroenke et al., 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11556941/).

Current redistribution and use must follow the official site terms:
[PHQ Screeners terms](https://www.phqscreeners.com/terms).

### GAD-7

The original criterion study included adults from United States primary-care
clinics, with independent mental-health interviews in a subset. It does not
make every population, language, or setting interchangeable:
[Spitzer et al., 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16717171/).

Current redistribution and use must follow the official site terms:
[PHQ Screeners terms](https://www.phqscreeners.com/terms).

### AUDIT

WHO supplies the manual and available language versions under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0
IGO. The documented starting setting is primary healthcare, and current
thresholds, language, population fit, and reuse terms require verification:
[WHO AUDIT manual and language versions](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-MSD-MSB-01.6a).

## Cross-instrument limits

Questionnaires can overestimate disorder prevalence compared with diagnostic
interviews, reinforcing the screening-versus-diagnosis boundary:
[Levis et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30894161/).

No sexuality-specific questionnaire is pre-cleared. No questionnaire can
reveal a person's true orientation, consent, motive, or diagnosis.

## Update triggers

- Changes to official versions, translations, scoring, terms, or rights.
- New validation or major review evidence affecting the populations in which a
  listed measure can be used.
- Addition or removal of a measure from the runtime registry.
