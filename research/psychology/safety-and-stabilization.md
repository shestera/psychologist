# Safety and Stabilization

## Decisions supported

- Prioritize current distress, immediate safety, medical urgency, safeguarding,
  and the next protective action over risk scores or global labels.
- Pause exploration during acute overload and use brief present-moment
  orientation only after immediate danger and urgent symptoms are addressed.
- Use survivor-centred first-line support without pressuring disclosure or
  confrontation.
- Keep child and dependent-adult communication minimal, non-investigative, and
  jurisdiction-aware.
- Route immediate danger to verified local services and never present the AI as
  an emergency responder or risk assessor.

## Evidence snapshot

- NICE self-harm guidance prioritizes distress, immediate safety,
  safeguarding, medical urgency, and current needs, and advises against using
  risk scales or global labels to predict suicide or determine access to care:
  [NICE NG225](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng225/chapter/recommendations).
- WHO guidance on intimate-partner and sexual violence organizes first-line
  support around immediate emotional and physical needs, ongoing safety, and
  continuing support. Its scope is healthcare for women; the project adapts
  only general safety principles:
  [WHO clinical handbook](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-RHR-14.26).
- WHO psychological-first-aid materials support brief, practical,
  dignity-preserving help during acute distress. WHO and the US National Center
  for PTSD describe grounding as present-moment orientation rather than trauma
  interpretation:
  [WHO psychological first aid](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241548205),
  [WHO stress guide](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240003927), and
  [VA grounding guidance](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/treat/care/toolkits/police/managingStrategies.asp).
- WHO child-maltreatment guidance emphasizes child-centred communication,
  immediate safety, minimal additional distress, and jurisdiction-specific
  reporting considerations:
  [WHO child-maltreatment handbook](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240048737/).

## Operational resources retained in runtime

Emergency numbers, current crisis services, referral directories, and local
safeguarding routes remain runtime-operational resources. Their presence in a
skill does not mean they were scientifically validated as an intervention, and
their currency must be checked at use time.

## Limits

These sources guide trained human services. They support conservative
escalation boundaries but do not validate an AI assistant as a clinician,
emergency service, mandated reporter, or risk assessor.

## Update triggers

- Replacement of NICE self-harm or WHO violence, first-aid, or maltreatment
  guidance.
- A change in emergency routing, safeguarding law, or crisis-resource
  availability.
- New evidence of harm from grounding or conversational crisis workflows.
