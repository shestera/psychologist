# Sexology Foundations and Formulation Methods

## Decisions supported

- Use a positive, rights-aware, non-pathologizing view of consensual adult
  sexuality while preserving consent, safety, and medical triage.
- Keep attraction, fantasy, arousal, behavior, identity, agreements, and values
  distinct.
- Treat asexuality and aromanticism as possible identities rather than low
  desire categories.
- Require impaired control plus distress or impairment before treating high
  sexual activity as potentially consistent with CSBD; moral distress alone is
  insufficient.
- Use responsive desire, dual control, arousal non-concordance, and life-course
  maps as optional psychoeducation, not diagnostic tests or universal models.
- Prohibit conversion practices and suggestive memory-recovery techniques.

## Foundational evidence

- WHO frames sexual health as physical, emotional, mental, and social
  well-being involving pleasure, safety, respect, rights, and freedom from
  coercion, discrimination, and violence:
  [WHO sexual health](https://www.who.int/health-topics/sexual-health).
- WHO describes sexual health across the life course and affirms that people
  with disabilities have the same sexual and reproductive health needs as
  others:
  [WHO life-course overview](https://www.who.int/news/item/11-02-2022-redefining-sexual-health-for-benefits-throughout-life)
  and [WHO disability guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241598682).
- APA guidance includes asexuality among sexual-minority orientations, and
  research distinguishes romantic from sexual orientation:
  [APA sexual-minority practice guidelines](https://www.apa.org/about/policy/psychological-sexual-minority-persons.pdf)
  and [Antonsen et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32095971/).
- The ICD-11 revision integrates biological and psychological contributors to
  sexual dysfunction, removes sexual-orientation categories, moves gender
  incongruence outside mental disorders, and distinguishes private consensual
  interests from clinically or publicly relevant paraphilic disorders:
  [Reed et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27717275/) and
  [WHO gender-incongruence FAQ](https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/frequently-asked-questions/gender-incongruence-and-transgender-health-in-the-icd).
- ICD-11 guidance for compulsive sexual behavior emphasizes persistent impaired
  control, distress or impairment, and differential assessment. High sexual
  interest and distress based only on moral judgment are insufficient:
  [Kraus et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5775124/).
- Many fantasies commonly described as unusual appear in general-population
  samples. This supports caution about labels, not a conclusion about safety,
  consent, or an individual behavior:
  [Joyal et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25359122/).
- The International Consultation on Sexual Medicine supports biopsychosocial,
  individualized assessment while acknowledging expert opinion where direct
  evidence is absent:
  [Brotto et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39786497/).
- Patient-centred, nonstigmatizing, trauma-informed sexual-history inquiry is
  supported in clinical contexts; the CDC Five Ps are not a mandatory AI
  interview:
  [Hong et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38331478/) and
  [CDC sexual-history guide](https://www.cdc.gov/sti/hcp/clinical-guidance/taking-a-sexual-history.html).
- Internal willingness and communicated consent vary by behavior and context;
  neither can be safely inferred from the other:
  [Willis et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34625009/).
- The ESSM position statement treats desire discrepancy as relative and dyadic,
  while rating its recommendations as opinion-based because direct evidence is
  scarce:
  [Dewitte et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32192965/).
- Psychological interventions show average benefits for some sexual
  dysfunctions, with substantial variation and limited comparative and
  long-term evidence:
  [Fruehauf et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23559141/).
- Reviews describe sexual-orientation and gender-identity change efforts as
  discredited and associated with serious harm:
  [Comer et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38937042/) and
  [Davison and Walden, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38237038/).
- Suggestive statements, imagery, and false feedback can induce false
  autobiographical beliefs or memories in experimental settings:
  [Muschalla and Schoenborn, 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33586291/).

## Formulation-model evidence

- Basson's circular model highlights responsive desire and overlapping phases,
  while later population work supports heterogeneous rather than universal
  sequences:
  [Basson, 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11898699/) and
  [Nowosielski et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26601676/).
- The dual control model describes sexual response as a balance of excitation
  and inhibition, with variation in measurement and application:
  [Janssen and Bancroft, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37267113/).
- Laboratory evidence shows imperfect agreement between genital and
  self-reported arousal, supporting separation of physiology, experience, and
  consent rather than individual prediction from group averages:
  [Chivers et al., 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20049519/).

## Limits

Most evidence concerns assessment or treatment by trained human professionals,
and some guidance combines evidence review with expert consensus. It supports
careful psychoeducation and conservative guardrails, not AI diagnostic accuracy
or effectiveness for an individual user.

## Update triggers

- Changes to ICD, WHO, APA, or specialist consensus relevant to sexuality.
- Material new evidence on desire models, arousal concordance, CSBD, conversion
  practices, or false-memory risk.
- Any change to consent, identity, or non-pathologizing instructions.
